How Netanyahu's coalition went from 64 seats to 53

Most of the drop is not voters abandoning the coalition. Seven of the eleven seats come from changes on the other side: wasted 2022 votes that counted this time, and a surge in Arab turnout.

How it was calculated

Coalition means Likud, Religious Zionism, Shas, UTJ and Otzma Yehudit. Jewish Home and Economic Freedom (2022) are not counted.

The first two steps are exact recounts. The 2022 votes are counted as if Meretz and Balad had passed the threshold, which gives the coalition 60. The 2026 votes are counted with Arab turnout at its 2022 rate, which gives it 56 instead of 53.

The last four are estimates. Ballots are secret, so voter movements are estimated by comparing about 4,000 polling places that exist in both elections and finding the movements that best explain the local changes. First-time voters are estimated from population data. The four effects are scaled to add up to the 4 seats.

Uncertainty. Under different assumptions the estimates range: wasted votes −0.4 to −1.2, turnout −1.7 to −2.2, first-time voters 0 to −0.5, switching −0.6 to −1.5.

Source: Central Elections Committee, results by polling place (2022, 2026). Seats allocated with the 3.25% threshold and the surplus-vote agreements. The 2026 results are formally published by 4 Nov 2026 and may still change slightly.
