How it was calculated

Blocs. Coalition: Likud, Religious Zionism, Shas, UTJ, Otzma Yehudit (Jewish Home and Economic Freedom in 2022 are not counted). Opposition: Yesh Atid, National Unity, Yisrael Beiteinu, Labor and Meretz in 2022; Yashar, Beyachad, the Democrats and Yisrael Beiteinu in 2026. Arab lists: Ra'am, Hadash-Ta'al, Balad, the Joint List.

The first two steps are exact recounts. The 2022 votes are counted with Meretz inside Labor and Balad inside Hadash-Ta'al, as they ran in 2026, which gives the coalition 60. The 2026 votes are counted with Arab turnout at its 2022 rate, which gives it 56 instead of 53.

The last three are estimates. Ballots are secret, so voter movements are estimated by comparing about 4,000 polling places that exist in both elections and finding the movements that best explain the local changes. First-time voters are estimated from population data. The three effects are scaled to add up to the 4 seats. In whole seats these 4 move from the coalition to the opposition, so the opposition's bars mirror the coalition's and the Arab lists' are zero.

Uncertainty. Under different assumptions the estimates range: wasted votes −0.4 to −1.2, turnout gap −1.9 to −2.3, switching −0.6 to −1.5.

Source: Central Elections Committee, results by polling place (2022, 2026). Seats allocated with the 3.25% threshold and the surplus-vote agreements. The 2026 results are formally published by 4 Nov 2026 and may still change slightly.
